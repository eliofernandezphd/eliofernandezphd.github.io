Interdisciplinary & Transdisciplinary Research

Social and quantitative sciences, working on the same problems.

Environmental problems cut across fields. I draw on the social sciences to understand people, policy, and power, and on the quantitative sciences to measure what’s happening on the ground.

Transdisciplinary work goes one step further, bringing in the knowledge of communities, agencies, and practitioners outside academia so the research answers questions people in Florida are actually asking.

A Short History of Interdisciplinary & Transdisciplinary Research

Working across fields is an old idea, but it took off as researchers faced problems no single discipline could solve.

Show the timelineHide the timeline 9 moments, 1959–2012
  1. Two cultures
  2. 1959

    The Two Cultures

    C. P. Snow warns that the sciences and humanities have stopped talking to each other.

  3. 1970

    Transdisciplinarity is named

    Jean Piaget uses the term at an OECD seminar on interdisciplinary teaching and research.

  4. Problems that cross boundaries
  5. 1973

    Wicked problems

    Horst Rittel and Melvin Webber argue planning problems have no single right answer and can't be solved by one discipline.

  6. 1984

    Complexity science

    The Santa Fe Institute is founded to study complex systems across physics, biology, economics, and social science.

  7. 1994

    Mode 2 knowledge

    Michael Gibbons and colleagues describe research that is organized around real-world problems instead of disciplines.

  8. Science with society
  9. 2001

    Sustainability science

    Robert Kates and colleagues call for a new field linking nature and society to support sustainable development.

  10. 2004

    Facilitating Interdisciplinary Research

    The U.S. National Academies recommend ways for universities and funders to support work across fields.

  11. 2009

    Social-ecological systems

    Elinor Ostrom publishes a framework for studying how people and ecosystems shape each other.

  12. 2012

    Transdisciplinary practice

    Daniel Lang and colleagues set out principles for research that includes communities and practitioners from the start.
